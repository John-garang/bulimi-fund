About Us
Trust-building copy about Obulimi?s purpose and direction.
Our story
Obulimi began with a practical observation: rural farming families can create lasting value from their land, but new cash-crop opportunities often fail when farmers receive one-off inputs without the knowledge, field support or buyer relationships needed to succeed. Obulimi was created to close those gaps.
We are introducing organized cocoa production in new cocoa-growing areas, beginning in Kyenjojo District. Our role is not simply to distribute seedlings. We register and train farmers, guide garden establishment, manage nursery production, provide continuing extension, improve access to appropriate inputs and connect quality dried cocoa beans to exporters.
Why cocoa
Cocoa is a long-term perennial crop with the potential to diversify household income and connect smallholder farmers to established regional and international value chains. That opportunity must be developed carefully: with suitable land, healthy planting material, shade and soil management, disciplined farm care, quality post-harvest handling and credible markets.
Where we work
Obulimi?s first pilot is planned for selected communities in Kyenjojo District. Expansion to neighbouring districts?including Kabarole, Kamwenge and Ibanda?will follow only after evidence from the pilot, local engagement, land suitability and operational capacity support responsible growth.
Our promise
We will build with farmers, measure honestly, protect people and land, and strengthen market relationships that reward quality and consistency.

Our Model
An end-to-end system from farmer qualification to market.
1. Register and qualify
We register farmers through parish-level groups, confirm that each farmer can dedicate at least one acre, map proposed plots and assess basic suitability, access and land-use safeguards.
2. Train and prepare
Farmers learn cocoa production fundamentals and receive practical guidance on land clearing, shade planning, planting-hole preparation, soil and water management and farm records.
3. Supply seedlings and establish
Obulimi manages nursery beds to produce high-quality cocoa seedlings, coordinates delivery for planting, supports correct field establishment and plans gap filling where required.
4. Extend and equip
Field teams provide continuing advice on mulching, pruning, shade, soil nutrition, sanitation, pest and disease management, safe input use and farm progress.
5. Buy and aggregate
As farms produce, Obulimi purchases quality dried cocoa beans from participating farmers, consolidates volumes and applies clear quality requirements.
6. Connect to exporters
Aggregated cocoa is sold to credible exporters, creating a route to market and greater opportunity to negotiate through organized volume, quality and traceability.
What farmers commit
Dedicate at least one suitable acre and demonstrate the right to use the land.
Attend training, prepare the plot and follow agreed establishment guidance.
Care for seedlings, maintain records and allow agreed extension visits.
Follow safe, responsible farm and post-harvest practices.
Participate in their local Obulimi farmer group and communicate challenges early.

Market Access
The commercial pathway that connects farmer effort to income.
How the buying model works
Farmers harvest and handle cocoa using Obulimi?s quality guidance. Obulimi checks eligible dried beans, records purchases, aggregates volumes and markets the cocoa to exporters. Organized supply can reduce the distance between farmer and buyer, improve quality consistency and strengthen bargaining through volume and information.
What farmers can expect
Quality requirements explained before sale.
Transparent weighing and purchase records.
Prices communicated clearly at the time of purchase.
Prompt payment through approved and secure payment methods.
Feedback where beans do not meet the agreed quality standard.
Basic cocoa quality requirements
Harvest only mature pods and follow the advised post-harvest process.
Ferment according to Obulimi?s current guidance where required.
Dry beans thoroughly on a clean, raised or approved surface.
Keep beans free from mould, smoke, chemicals, stones and other foreign matter.
Store in clean, dry bags and protect from moisture before sale.
For exporters and cocoa buyers
Obulimi is developing a traceable supply base in new cocoa-growing areas. We welcome buyer relationships that support clear specifications, quality improvement, responsible sourcing, dependable offtake and transparent commercial terms. Early buyer engagement can help align farmer training and post-harvest systems with market needs.

Frequently Asked Questions
Plain-language answers for farmers, partners and supporters.
What is Obulimi?
Obulimi is a Ugandan social enterprise that helps smallholder farmers establish and manage cocoa farms, access quality planting material and inputs, and connect dried cocoa beans to markets.
Where does Obulimi operate?
The first pilot is planned in selected communities in Kyenjojo District. Future expansion to neighbouring districts will depend on pilot evidence, suitability, local engagement and operational capacity.
Who can join as a farmer?
Eligibility depends on being in a current operating area, dedicating at least one suitable acre, having a clear right to use the land, attending training and committing to maintain the garden.
Why is the minimum one acre?
A minimum area helps make training, extension, logistics, records and future aggregation practical while creating a meaningful long-term farm enterprise. Final acceptance still depends on plot suitability and programme capacity.
Does every registered farmer automatically receive seedlings?
No. Registration expresses and records interest. Seedling allocation depends on eligibility, plot preparation, training completion, planting schedules, nursery supply and the terms communicated for that season.
What training and extension will farmers receive?
Training covers cocoa establishment and farm management, including land preparation, planting, shade, soil care, pruning, sanitation, pest and disease management, safe input use, harvesting, post-harvest quality and records. Field support continues according to the programme schedule.
Does Obulimi provide free inputs?
Obulimi plans to improve last-mile access to selected organic fertilizers and organic pesticides at subsidised prices where available. Product availability, farmer contribution and terms will be communicated clearly; not every input is free.

Frequently Asked Questions
Continued.
Will Obulimi buy farmers? cocoa?
Obulimi?s market-access model is designed to purchase quality dried cocoa beans from participating farmers, aggregate volumes and sell to exporters. Purchases will depend on quality, operating capacity, current market conditions and published buying arrangements.
What quality does Obulimi buy?
Beans must meet the quality requirements communicated for the buying period, including appropriate fermentation where required, thorough drying, cleanliness and freedom from mould, smoke and foreign matter.
Does Obulimi guarantee the highest price?
No responsible buyer can guarantee the highest price at all times. Obulimi aims for competitive, transparent pricing and stronger market access through quality, organized volume and buyer relationships.
Does using organic fertilizer mean the cocoa is certified organic?
No. Using an organic input does not by itself create organic certification. Certification requires a defined standard, approved systems, records and independent verification. Obulimi will only claim certification when it is formally achieved.
When will farmers harvest cocoa?
Cocoa commonly begins producing around the third year under suitable conditions and good management, although timing and yield vary by planting material, weather, soil and farm care. Obulimi plans around a multi-year establishment period and does not guarantee a specific harvest date.
How can I donate?
Use the verified donation options on the Donate page, or contact Obulimi for institutional and in-kind support. Do not send funds to a number or account that is not published through an official Obulimi channel.
How can a company partner with Obulimi?
Companies and institutions can support nurseries, farmer training, extension, inputs, technology, measurement, responsible sourcing, quality systems and market access. Submit the partnership form so the relevant team can follow up.
How will Obulimi protect farmers and the environment?
Obulimi intends to screen plots against forest, protected land and wetlands; confirm land-use consent; promote safe inputs and integrated pest management; protect farmer data; prevent child labour; and encourage participation by women and young people.
