Frequently Asked Questions

Plain-language answers for farmers, partners and supporters.

What is Bulimi?

Bulimi is a Ugandan social enterprise that helps smallholder farmers establish and manage cocoa farms, access quality planting material and inputs, and connect dried cocoa beans to markets.

Where does Bulimi operate?

The first pilot is planned in selected communities in Kyenjojo District. Future expansion to neighbouring districts will depend on pilot evidence, suitability, local engagement and operational capacity.

Who can join as a farmer?

Eligibility depends on being in a current operating area, dedicating at least one suitable acre, having a clear right to use the land, attending training and committing to maintain the garden.

Why is the minimum one acre?

A minimum area helps make training, extension, logistics, records and future aggregation practical while creating a meaningful long-term farm enterprise. Final acceptance still depends on plot suitability and programme capacity.

Does every registered farmer automatically receive seedlings?

No. Registration expresses and records interest. Seedling allocation depends on eligibility, plot preparation, training completion, planting schedules, nursery supply and the terms communicated for that season.

What training and extension will farmers receive?

Training covers cocoa establishment and farm management, including land preparation, planting, shade, soil care, pruning, sanitation, pest and disease management, safe input use, harvesting, post-harvest quality and records. Field support continues according to the programme schedule.

Does Bulimi provide free inputs?

Bulimi plans to improve last-mile access to selected organic fertilizers and organic pesticides at subsidised prices where available. Product availability, farmer contribution and terms will be communicated clearly; not every input is free.

Will Bulimi buy farmers' cocoa?

Bulimi's market-access model is designed to purchase quality dried cocoa beans from participating farmers, aggregate volumes and sell to exporters. Purchases will depend on quality, operating capacity, current market conditions and published buying arrangements.

What quality does Bulimi buy?

Beans must meet the quality requirements communicated for the buying period, including appropriate fermentation where required, thorough drying, cleanliness and freedom from mould, smoke and foreign matter.

Does Bulimi guarantee the highest price?

No responsible buyer can guarantee the highest price at all times. Bulimi aims for competitive, transparent pricing and stronger market access through quality, organized volume and buyer relationships.

Does using organic fertilizer mean the cocoa is certified organic?

No. Using an organic input does not by itself create organic certification. Certification requires a defined standard, approved systems, records and independent verification. Bulimi will only claim certification when it is formally achieved.

When will farmers harvest cocoa?

Cocoa commonly begins producing around the third year under suitable conditions and good management, although timing and yield vary by planting material, weather, soil and farm care. Bulimi plans around a multi-year establishment period and does not guarantee a specific harvest date.

How can I donate?

Use the verified donation options on the Donate page, or contact Bulimi for institutional and in-kind support. Do not send funds to a number or account that is not published through an official Bulimi channel.

How can a company partner with Bulimi?

Companies and institutions can support nurseries, farmer training, extension, inputs, technology, measurement, responsible sourcing, quality systems and market access. Submit the partnership form so the relevant team can follow up.

How will Bulimi protect farmers and the environment?

Bulimi intends to screen plots against forest, protected land and wetlands; confirm land-use consent; promote safe inputs and integrated pest management; protect farmer data; prevent child labour; and encourage participation by women and young people.
